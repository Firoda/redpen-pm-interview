Open on your feet. Two hours, and this is the session where you talk least.
Say it plainly at the top: "Today is mostly you working. I teach for about forty-five minutes in total. The rest is two exercises and a vote." That expectation changes how the room sits.
The session has two halves that look unrelated and are not. The first is the organisation around your product: you are responsible for an outcome and have authority over almost nobody who produces it. The second is the choice your product makes. The bridge is the same idea twice - decide, then explain the trade to whoever lost.
Ninety seconds, no more. If you are behind at the start of a workshop session you never recover it, and the workshop is the point.

===== ppt/notesSlides/notesSlide10.xml =====
Use the divider as a breath. Twenty-three minutes of teaching, then they work.
The line on the right of this slide sets up the block. Most of this room will not be product managers. Almost all of them will spend their careers accountable for results produced by people they cannot instruct - consulting, banking, family business, general management. Product management is simply the job where that condition is most naked, which is why it earns twenty minutes of an MBA elective.
Four moves in the block: what you actually control; how product organisations are shaped and why the same person thrives in one shape and fails in another; what each function needs from you; and what to do when the disagreement does not resolve.
Foreshadow the ending now so it does not feel like a swerve: in a five-person team with no hierarchy, the tie-breaker at eleven at night is whoever is most stubborn. The block closes by making them fix that.

===== ppt/slides/slide14.xml =====
Goals and Non-Goals for your MVP 
14
Goals
Identify the goals that you want to achieve. P0 goals need to be met when this feature/solution is released in MVP1. P1 goals are desirable but may be cut to meet schedule deadlines or for other reasons, and can go in later MVPs..
Non-Goals
Non-goals are things you are explicitly not doing or supporting. This does not mean that you are actively trying to prevent them from occurring – it simply means that you are not actively working toward enabling them during this release
