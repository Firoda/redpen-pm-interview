Open on your feet. Two hours, and this is the session where you talk least.
Say it plainly at the top: "Today is mostly you working. I teach for about forty-five minutes in total. The rest is two exercises and a vote." That expectation changes how the room sits.
The session has two halves that look unrelated and are not. The first is the organisation around your product: you are responsible for an outcome and have authority over almost nobody who produces it. The second is the choice your product makes. The bridge is the same idea twice - decide, then explain the trade to whoever lost.
Ninety seconds, no more. If you are behind at the start of a workshop session you never recover it, and the workshop is the point.

Short description of the product Idea you are working on -
An always-available PM mock interviewer for ISB PGP students in placement season. Candidates take voice or text mocks drawn from curated PM resources and their own casebooks (product design, RCA, metrics, guesstimates, strategy, tech concepts), get strict, rubric-based feedback at the end of every mock, and see their scores tracked topic by topic, with nudges for neglected topics. Unlike general AI, it doesn't praise and forget; unlike peers, it needs no booking. The MVP serves Ishita (the mock-heavy PM aspirant); Rohan (the short-notice multi-tracker) follows in Phase 2.

Goal 1 - Honest, role-aware feedback
3
Goal — in their words
Action
Metric (per what, per when)
What would you evaluate
“Tell me honestly what’s weak in my answer, for a PM role, so I can fix it before the real round.”
Persona: Ishita, the mock-heavy candidate
JTBD 1 · Problem P1 · raised by 18 of 24
“AI tools are a bit submissive”
• Completes a full mock: the question plus follow-ups
• Opens the end-of-mock feedback: five rubric scores and 3 fixes quoting her words
• Rates it: “Was this specific and honest?”
• Re-attempts the question or a variant to apply a fix
Feedback read rate
% of completed mocks whose feedback is opened to the fixes · per mock, weekly
Feedback trust score
% of mocks rated 4–5 on “specific and honest” · per mock, weekly
Fix-applied rate
% of mocks followed by a re-attempt within 72 h · per user, weekly
Fix uplift
Avg score change on the flagged dimension, attempt vs re-attempt · per re-attempt
• Keep fix uplift: it shows the feedback was understood and used, not just read
• Read rate alone is a vanity check: opening is not trusting
• Guardrail, calibration: on a sample, AI scores within ±1 of a senior or alumni mock. Too lenient repeats the ChatGPT problem; too harsh and users quit
• Guardrail: feedback within 2 min
• Each week, ask 5 users what they would change in the feedback
Why do you think this is a Goal for the User ?
Honest feedback was the most common pain: 18 of 24 candidates raised it. General AI “is generally like, yeah, you’re doing good”, peers flatter or aren’t free, and interviewers never say why a round went wrong. Candidates lose rounds on answers that felt fine, and pay for strict prompts, a case bot or ₹50,000 coaching to get it. If our feedback isn’t trusted, nothing else in the product matters.

Strategy – Why do you think your solution will win ? 
7
We win on the gap no option fills today: honest feedback, on demand, that remembers.
Where we play
Now: ISB PGP students preparing for PM interviews in placement season (~135 first users this term)
Next: consulting and GM aspirants with the same pain (~650), on the same engine
Later: working professionals switching roles (Vikram), other B-schools
What would make us lose
General AI adds memory and a strict mode. Our answer: a PM-specific rubric calibrated against senior mocks, and campus distribution it won’t chase.
How we win against what they use today
Strict by default
Rubric built in (structure, user focus, prioritisation, metrics, communication). General AI is lenient unless prompted hard: “I don’t know what the right prompt is”
Remembers
Every mock adds up into topic scores. ChatGPT loses context by the 2nd–3rd case; error logs lose most feedback
Always available
Voice or text, no booking. Seniors are booked “15 days in advance”
One trusted PM library
Curated questions, case variants and your own casebooks, instead of scattered material
Why it gets harder to copy
• History as switching cost: a season of scored mocks and weak topics stays with us
• Calibration data: each senior-vs-AI comparison sharpens the rubric
• Campus density: spreads through section and PM-prep WhatsApp groups; one cohort, one season
• Phase 2 case-buddy matching adds a peer network that a general chatbot doesn’t have

What is your MVP Scope ? 
8
Success test: at least 40% of first-time users complete a second mock within 7 days.
In scope: MVP1 (must ship)
1. Topic and mode: 6 PM topics (product design, RCA, metrics, guesstimates, strategy, tech concepts); voice or text, switchable mid-session; 15+ questions per topic
2. Own uploads: questions or casebooks, visible only to the user
3. Full mock: follow-ups that respond to the answer; case variants that change the user, market or constraint
4. Feedback within 2 min: 1–5 score with a reason on 5 dimensions, plus 3 fixes quoting the user’s words
5. Progress tracker: score trend per topic, weakest topic highlighted
6. Home dashboard: nudge when a topic is unpractised for 7 days or feedback is unread
Out of scope
Phase 2: case-buddy matching (P4); JD and company tailoring (P2, Rohan)
Later: consulting, GM and finance tracks; payments
Not building: video or body-language analysis; resume and application tools; writing answers for the user; live in-mock coaching; replacing human mocks
How we build and test it
Prototype on an LLM in a prototyping tool, seeded with a curated PM question set. Nudges sent by the team on WhatsApp (Wizard of Oz). 20 PM aspirants outside our circle, 14 days, ₹0.
Tracked from day one
Events behind the NSM and goal metrics:
• mock_started / mock_completed
• feedback_opened / feedback_rated
• question_reattempted
• tracker_viewed
• nudge_sent / nudge_acted
These give Weekly Reviewed Mocks, second-mock rate, feedback trust and fix uplift without any extra build.

Why do you believe that your MVP scope is sufficiently useful for your Consumer ? 
9
Useful enough means they come back without being asked: a second mock within 7 days.
It does the top job end to end
A full mock plus strict feedback solves P1, the most common pain (18 of 24), on first use. No prompt setup needed.
It replaces a patchwork they already pay for
Today they combine strict prompts, a paid case bot, manual error logs and booking seniors. Spend seen: ChatGPT Go (~₹400 a month), Claude (~₹25,000 a year), a ₹50,000 coaching package. The MVP does feedback, practice and memory in one place.
It fits how they prep
Several mocks a week, 1–2 hours a day around classes: one 20–30 minute mock fits a free slot.
It is narrow enough to be good
PM only: “for product there are four or five types of questions”. One library can be curated well, and 5 of our 24 candidates target PM.
What we left out doesn’t block the job
• JD tailoring mainly helps Rohan, our Phase 2 persona
• Buddy matching complements peer mocks, it isn’t needed to practise
• Video wasn’t asked for; realism (8 of 24) is covered by voice
We will know fast
If fewer than 40% come back for a second mock within 7 days, we test JD and company tailoring (P2) as the stronger hook.
